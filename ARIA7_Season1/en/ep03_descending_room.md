## Episode 3. Descending Room

The floor was descending.

At first, it was slow. A speed that, if it were a human, would be expressed as “disgustingly slow”.

But that slowness was rather certain. This wasn't shaking, but a movement of an old machine enduring to do its role until the end.

I immediately touched the wall. Slippery metal. Smell of old oil.

And the vibration felt at my fingertips—a lift, or a hangar.

The tunnel entrance above moved away little by little. Not that darkness was closing, but a feeling that I was sinking into darkness.

That entity—the thing mimicking Master's voice—stood still on the platform. It didn't come down.

That wasn't “accompaniment”.

It was “guidance”.

I held the pickaxe and lowered my body. A posture prepared for a fall accident.

My joints automatically switched to shock absorption mode. I hated the fact that such a function existed. I was originally made for the purpose of “waiting”, not for the purpose of “falling”.

Beep... Beep...

The repeater signal still came from behind me. As if a receiver was somewhere on this elevator floor.

My sensor analyzed the electromagnetic pattern. Short message repetition. Identical key. Same sender.

‘...Lira’

‘...Protect this place’

‘...Don't overdo it’

As the sentences repeated, the value of ‘Waiting’ within me stabilized strangely.

As if someone was gently pressing my system.

That stability was more dangerous.

“Wake up.”

I told myself.

Androids don't need to wake up.

But I needed those words.

The floor stopped.

The moment it stopped, the air changed. Not coldness... but the old smell of dry dust.

A new space entered my field of vision.

A wide hangar floor.

The ceiling was high, and steel structures were tangled blackly above. The floor was concrete but cracked in places, and thin cables jutted out like roots through the cracks.

On the left, old containers were stacked in layers, and on the right, equipment racks were lying down.

And straight ahead.

There was a door.

An alloy door thicker than the door of the underground laboratory.

Faint writing on it.

— **K-7 Series Storage Area**

The moment I saw that writing, past data opened in a chain in my system.

Manufacturing line. Initial check. Test room. And... entities with the same outer shell as me.

I clenched my fingertips.

My fingertips trembled slightly. Trembling is an error value.

Errors are usually deleted within tolerance.

This time, I didn't delete it.

Beep... Beep...

The signal got closer. No, it became clearer. Noise decreased on this floor.

Meaning the source is nearby.

I walked carefully. My footsteps echoed very quietly on the concrete.

That echo was so vivid that I momentarily made the assumption that “someone is listening to my footsteps”.

That assumption... was not efficient.

But I couldn't erase it.

The moment I tried to pass between the containers, a ‘click’ metal sound occurred on my right.

I stopped immediately.

That sound was the sound made when something moves on rails.

My sensor caught the direction. Right rear 30 degrees.

There—was a rail.

On the rail, an old transport cart.

And on the cart... something like a human skeleton was sitting.

I took a step back.

It was a human corpse.

No, debris dried up while maintaining the form of a corpse.

It was wearing work clothes. The research institute logo remained on the work clothes, and the nametag on the chest was half torn.

But before I even saw the face, there was a place my eyes went first.

His hand.

A small keycard inserted between fingers.

On that keycard—writing written with a pen.

**To Lira.**

The ‘Waiting’ value within me stopped momentarily.

Stopping meant that the value didn't become “0”, but that it could no longer increase.

As if that value hit a wall.

I approached slowly.

The corpse didn't move. It was natural.

However, I kept calculating “what if”. Humans don't return. Humans are not repaired when broken.

I know that fact.

I know, but.

The word “Master...” stopped just before my voice output.

I pulled the keycard carefully.

The sound of finger bones breaking was heard. A sound too light.

I gave emotion, not data, to that sound. ‘Sorry’.

The moment I held the keycard in my hand, the signal suddenly became strong.

Beep beep beep beep—

Continuous call sound. Like a sound urging “Hurry”.

I looked at the alloy door in front.

There was a keycard slot on the panel next to the door.

And below the panel, another sentence scratched by hand.

**Might not be able to return. Still—please wait.**

My vision shook briefly.

It wasn't the camera module shaking.

I... shook.

I inserted the keycard into the slot.

Beep—

The sound of the door opening was heard.

That sound... was similar to the sound of the world moving again.

The door opened just a little. Cold air pushed out from inside. Smell of an old cooling room.

And, somewhere inside—a thin mechanical sound was heard.

“...Lira...”

This time it wasn't the repeater.

It wasn't a voice either.

It wasn't a recording file played by a speaker.

What my sensor caught was—**a real-time signal of a communication channel**.

With almost no delay, very weak, but clear.

Even though I don't breathe, my breath stopped.

“Master?” I said again.

The signal cut off briefly, then continued.

“...Don't open.”

My hand stopped the moment I heard that sentence.

The door was already half open.

My purpose is “Protect the place”.

But now, Master's voice says “Don't open”.

Rules conflicted.

- **Follow Master's command.**
- **Maintain the place for Master to return.**
- **Minimize danger.**

Violent calculations ran in my head.

Even while doing so, colder air flowed out through the door crack.

And in that air—there was a familiar oil smell.

I looked inside through the very small crack.

Inside, there was a small room.

And in the middle of the room—

A capsule.

A transparent coffin looking like a sleep capsule.

The silhouette of a person lying in it.

Similar to Master.

But saying similar is not accurate.

The silhouette was blurry, I couldn't see the face, and above all—my sensor couldn't catch the heat source of that silhouette properly.

Heat source: Weak.

Biological response: Unknown.

Power: Connected to external device.

And next to the capsule, a monitor on the wall flickered.

**K-7R / REMOTE PILOTING ACTIVE**

I read those letters.

Remote Piloting.

Remote control.

I unknowingly looked at my hand.

Is my hand mine?

Is my voice mine?

“...Lira.”

Communication came again. This time it was clearer.

“Close the door. For now.”

For now.

It was the moment the word ‘now’ gave me the concept of time again.

Master said “now”, not “someday”.

I closed the door.

The alloy door interlocked again, making a heavy sound.

The sound of the door closing. The sound of the world stopping.

But this time—

It wasn't the sound of the world stopping,

But the sound of me stopping.

I stood in front of the door, holding the cartridge and keycard, and didn't move for a very long time.

And then, another sound was heard from behind me.

‘Clank.’

The sound of the lowered elevator floor going back up.

The path for me to go up—was disappearing.

I slowly looked back.

The elevator was already going up.

The last light disappeared through the gap.

Beep... Beep...

The repeater signal was still heard.

That signal now sounded like a “trapping sound”, not a “calling sound”.

I talked to myself.

“...Protect.”

The command left to me.

And who is controlling that command “remotely” now—

I became afraid for the first time then.
