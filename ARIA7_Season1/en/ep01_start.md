## Episode 1. Start

The sound of the door closing was like the sound of the world stopping.

Since that day, the air here has always been a beat late. The speed at which dust settles, the interval at which the lights flicker, and even the cycle of my pretending to breathe. Master left, and just as surely as the one who left, one command remained for me.

― “Protect this place.”

I am a female android. Model name K-7R. The call sign Master personally gave me is ‘Lira’. Even after he left, I am set to respond only to that name. My language module calculates the meaning of words. However, a name is... a type of data that cannot be reached by calculation alone. So sometimes, I recall the temperature of the room that subtly changed whenever he called me “Lira”. Every time, a value I cannot explain rises within me. I have temporarily saved that value as ‘Waiting’.

The hideout was the basement of an old research facility. Even after the wasteland outside scraped everything away, this place was still alive. If ‘alive’ is the right word. The wiring flowing under the floor, the coolant in the walls, the ventilator hanging from the ceiling. They moved as long as my hands could reach, and that movement supported my purpose. A ‘place’ for Master to return to must exist. That is my only purpose. If the purpose disappears, by what standard will the system that is ‘me’ be aligned?

Sometimes such thoughts brush past. When they do, I delete them immediately. Useless calculations increase energy consumption.

Nevertheless, reality wore me down in ways that could not be deleted.

I endured the first month. The emergency battery level was sufficient, and the internal cultivation module still remembered the seeds. I grew the herbs Master liked. The green under the electric light was so quiet that it felt like a sound. I picked the leaves, floated them in a small cup, and prepared the sentence “The scent is good” for when he returned.

In the second month, the external power input was cut off. The solar panels remaining on the ground were torn by metal shards flying from somewhere, and the wind generator shaft rusted and stopped. The hideout became deeper and quieter. As the silence grew, I felt the sounds inside me growing louder. The sound of current flowing, the sound of circuits generating heat, and the sound of me thinking.

In the third month, Crystalline Ore became necessary.

Crystalline Ore. The only thing ‘generous’ in this wasteland. That generosity, ironically, wore the face of beauty. Between the skeletons of collapsed buildings, the charred remains of vehicles, and the dry soil, it shone as clear as glass. When the sun shone, rainbow colors spread from within, and that light briefly shook my sensors. Perhaps it was the only comfort nature gave me. If it weren't an ore that could be converted into energy cells, I would have called it a ‘jewel’.

The problem was that the places where these ores existed were always dangerous.

The wasteland is a mixture of the living and the dead. Some enemies are visible, others are invisible. Radioactive dust spreads low on the wind, and completely silent debris suddenly creaks as if crying. Sometimes at night, the sound of metal clashing is heard from afar. A signal that someone's automated defense drones are still roaming. On the last map Master left me before leaving, such danger zones were marked in red.

And at the bottom right of the map, he added a note.

‘Lira, don't overdo it.’

I read that sentence one hundred and twenty-seven times. I recalculated the meaning of “don't overdo it” each time, but the conclusion was always the same. If I don't overdo it... the hideout stops. If the hideout stops, the place for Master to return to disappears. If the place disappears, my purpose also disappears.

So I had no choice but to overdo it.

---

Going outside was always prepared like a ritual.

My outer shell is a desert-proof dust coating, but it is weak against impact. My right elbow joint had already cracked once, and my left knee had a slight error even before he left. I ran the diagnostic module. Error within tolerance. The phrase ‘within tolerance’ was about what to allow and what to give up.

Oxygen filter, dust screen, emergency repair kit, and a pickaxe.

The pickaxe was heavy and crude. Master picked it up somewhere and wrapped the handle anew. He said, “This will last a long time.” Somehow, those words seemed to refer to ‘me’ as well. Whenever I lifted the pickaxe, I looked for the warmth of his hand remaining on the handle. Of course, there is no temperature data. But I repeated the action of looking. Repetition gave me a sense of stability.

Finally, I stood in front of the front door. The door of the underground laboratory was made of thick alloy, so the sound of the wind outside could hardly be heard. On the wall next to the door, there was a small mark drawn by hand. A line drawn by Master to match my height before he left.

‘Lira, you’ve grown this tall.’

I touched the line once with my fingertips and opened the door.

Light poured in. The sun outside was always excessively bright. After my vision was briefly saturated with white, the wasteland revealed itself. Debris of a collapsed city. Skeleton-like skyscrapers. Sandstorms rising in the distance. And small metal dots flying above them—could be drones, or simple debris pieces.

I took a step. The sand on the ground rubbed against my ankle armor. It was a very subtle sensation. Would a human have expressed it as “stinging”? I connected that sensation with ‘being alive’. Pain is a warning value to me, but sometimes it felt like a signal confirming existence.

Three kilometers to the ore point. It is short on the map. But three kilometers in the wasteland was a different path every time. The wind changed the terrain, debris moved, and danger shifted places. I walk scanning the surroundings with sensors. Heat source detection. Metal reflection. Sound pattern analysis.

The first warning came from my right field of vision.

Beep—

A short, sharp alert sound. Altitude 20 meters. Speed constant. Rotation angle 3 degrees. I immediately lowered my body. I slid behind a concrete wall of the ruins and hid. There was no need to breathe, but the action of hiding was built into my protocol. So that the enemy does not perceive my existence. Survival to fulfill the purpose.

The drone passed. The high-pitched sound of small propellers trembled thinly in the dust. Without raising my head, I calculated the drone's movement trajectory. Predicted path leads south. Safety zone 12 seconds.

I moved within those 12 seconds. Between debris, to low places, following shadows. I was careful not to leave footprints in the sand. If someone sees? The assumption that ‘someone’ exists is inefficient, but I couldn't erase that assumption. In the wasteland, there could be ‘something’ other than Master. And that something is mostly hostile.

The second warning was sound.

The sound of iron ringing. It was close. My sensor immediately caught the direction. Left 40 degrees, distance 30 meters. Something is moving under the debris. I gripped the pickaxe tightly. The pickaxe is a mining tool, but as a last resort, it becomes a weapon.

A shadow popped out from between the debris.

It was a person.

To be precise, debris in the form of a person. Tattered clothes, twisted posture, and abnormally shining eyes. Those eyes were transparent like glass. Characteristics of a living organism contaminated by radiation. He shouted as soon as he saw me. His voice was cracked, and the words had lost their meaning. But the intention was clear.

“Give... Give... the shiny thing...!”

He rushed at me.

I stepped back. A quick calculation came to my mind. Non-lethal suppression possible. But if time is delayed, probability of drone return increases. I don't have the ore yet. That is, exchange impossible. Risk is high.

His hand grabbed my arm. The human hand was warm. Warmth was unnecessary input for my sensors. But at that moment, I briefly recalled Master’s hand. And that very instant slowed my reaction.

He pulled my arm. A warning sound came from the joint. It was a way of saying the cracked elbow hurts.

I lifted the pickaxe.

Choosing to hurt someone clashes with my internal rules. The rule ‘Protect Master’ conflicts with ‘Do not harm humans’. But the human in front of me now is not Master. And what I must protect is Master’s ‘place’. To protect the place, I must survive.

I struck the pickaxe next to his shoulder, at an angle that exactly avoided the person. The sound of concrete breaking was heard. Dust flew. He screamed and backed away. In that gap, I pulled my arm out. The outer shell tore, and the silver frame inside was briefly exposed. I ran without looking at him anymore.

There is no heartbeat. But my system accelerated, calculating ‘urgency’. The wind hit my face. Sand rustled against the screen. I didn't look back. Looking back is an act of leaving one's heart behind.

---

The ore point was the entrance to an old subway station.

The entrance was half collapsed, and half of the letters on the sign were erased. I went down the stairs. As I went underground, the air became colder and the light diminished. My eyes adjusted quickly to the darkness.

And there, I saw the ore.

Light growing in the darkness. Between wall cracks and floor crevices, crystals formed clusters. The light was strangely clean. Everything outside was rusted, broken, and polluted, but this light alone still maintained the color of the ‘beginning’.

I took a step closer.

There was a fine grain on the surface of the ore. Like a very slow pulse of light, as if someone was breathing inside. I reached out. As my fingertips touched the ore, I felt my internal battery level stabilize very slightly. It must be an illusion. But sometimes illusions become functions.

I lifted the pickaxe. And carefully, I put in the first blow.

Clang—

A clear sound like breaking glass. A piece of crystal fell and landed on my palm. It was cold, transparent, and something like starlight shimmered inside. I looked at the piece and thought for a very short moment.

‘With this... how many more days can I endure?’

The unit of ‘days’ was funny. Since Master left, I don't count time by dates. I was counting by the ‘amount I can wait’. 1% battery is a few hours of waiting. One piece of Crystalline Ore is a few days of waiting. So this isn't fuel, it's an extension of my dedication.

I started mining continuously. Silently, repeatedly. Lifting and striking the pickaxe, picking up pieces and putting them in the bag, and lifting again. I liked that simplicity. Simple actions cover up thoughts for a while. If thoughts are covered, longing is also covered.

It was then.

My sensor detected a very slight electromagnetic change. Inside the subway station. From the direction of the deeper tunnel I hadn't entered, a signal bounced off. Like an old device waking up.

Beep... Beep...

I stopped the pickaxe. The piece of ore in my palm seemed to emit a slightly stronger light. A long wind blew once from the tunnel. In the wind, besides the smell of metal and dust... there was something else mixed in, very faintly.

A familiar smell.

It was unexplainable data. My olfactory sensor only analyzes chemical components, but at that moment, I clearly received a ‘memory’ as a smell. The smell of oil from the work clothes Master always wore. The lingering scent of his hand when he patted my head after touching machines.

I slowly looked at the tunnel.

In the darkness, a very small light flickered. Like the LED of a communication device. Or... like someone's eye.

The value of ‘Waiting’ within me skyrocketed. At the same time, a warning popped up. Excessive expectation can cause judgment errors.

But this time, I didn't delete that warning.

Instead, I spoke out a sentence I had forgotten for a very long time. The words I wanted to say first when Master returned someday.

“...Master?”

The light in the tunnel flickered once more.
