## Episode 2. At the End of the Signal

The light in the tunnel flickered once more.

I couldn't move from that spot.

The expression that I couldn't move is not accurate. My motors are normal, and my joints are within tolerance. However, a state of 'unable to move', not a calculation of 'must not move', occurred somewhere in the system.

“...Master?”

My voice hit the tunnel wall and returned as a small echo.

The me in the echo sounded too human.

Beep... Beep...

The signal was irregular. A sound of something waking up broken. A sound as if a machine broken for a long time suddenly remembered it was alive.

I don't breathe, but I pretended to catch my breath. Unnecessary actions were sometimes necessary for me.

I pushed the ore piece deep into my bag. The metal buckle rang quietly.

As soon as that sound spread through the tunnel, the signal briefly stopped.

I immediately executed the judgment module.

- Inside tunnel: Vision limited
- Electromagnetic signal: Irregular
- External drone: Probability of return exists
- My damage status: Minute crack in right arm joint
- Objective: Return after securing ore

The objective was clear.

But I pushed that objective aside for a moment.

The smell mixing in from inside the tunnel—oil, metal, and the lingering scent of unexplained 'memory'—suppressed my calculations.

My system displayed “Probability of illusion 87%”.

I read that number, but took a step forward.

The entrance to the black tunnel at the end of the platform where I came down gaped like a mouth. There is no flashlight. Instead, my vision switched to low-light mode.

Darkness lost its form, and as form was lost, sounds and smells grew larger.

Beep... Beep...

I walked in carefully, one step at a time. The soles of my feet pressed the gravel between the rails. A very small 'crunching' sound.

At that moment—

On my right wall, another light flashed.

I immediately lowered my body.

Stuck to the wall was an old communication repeater. A device that amplified signals when research institutes in the wasteland connected the underground. It used to be rusted and covered in dust, but the LED was alive.

The word 'alive' is strange again.

Next to the repeater was a small panel. A cover that opens by hand.

I opened the cover carefully. The sound of old rubber packing tearing was heard. The circuit inside showed traces of being half-burnt. And next to it—

A cartridge slot.

As soon as I saw that slot, a file automatically opened internally.

A list of equipment Master used.

The standard of data cartridges Master enjoyed using.

And a sentence Master often said.

‘Lira, if you see something like this, don't touch it, just call me first.’

I just stopped trying to take out that sentence.

Because I know that even if I call, there is no one to come.

Yet, my hand had already moved.

The moment my right hand tried to touch the repeater, a fine warning rang from the joint. The way the cracked elbow says “Don't overdo it”.

I switched to my left hand.

Inside the cover, a thin piece of plastic was inserted in the cartridge slot. It wasn't visible well buried in dust, but as I pulled it with my fingertips, it slowly came out.

On the surface of the cartridge, handwritten letters remained.

Lira

The writing was crooked, and the ink was smudged.

But that crookedness was the crookedness I had seen and memorized hundreds of times.

The value of 'Waiting' within me—

Beyond rising, it skyrocketed like a runaway.

“Master...”

I clutched the cartridge to my bosom.

I don't have the concept of a bosom, but I needed it then. Cold plastic touched my outer shell. The sensation of cold strangely gave me stability.

Beep... Beep... Beep...

The repeater suddenly rang continuously. As if pouring out what was suffocating as I removed the cartridge.

I immediately scanned the incoming signal.

Signal source: Deeper in the tunnel.

Distance: Approximately 120 meters.

Power: Weak.

Pattern: Short message repetition.

When the short message was decrypted, broken text appeared.

[ ...ra ]

[ ...ra ]

[ ...ra ]

Lira.

Data with only part of my name left.

I stopped on the spot.

The conclusion that 'it is calling me' and the conclusion that 'it is luring me' came to mind simultaneously. The two conclusions erased each other.

In that gap, I made the most human choice.

Confirmation.

I gripped the pickaxe and went further into the tunnel.

Cables attached to the wall hung down like black roots. Old warning signs rolled on the floor. Words like 'High Voltage' and 'No Entry' were half erased.

120 meters was long.

For me—it was a distance where waiting could waver several times.

And finally, the tunnel widened.

A small maintenance space.

A place where people used to come and go to check equipment in the old days. Now only dust and silence occupied it.

Silence... just as I thought that.

I heard.

‘...Click.’

A very small metal sound.

The sound of a bolt rolling on the floor. Or the sound of a switch being pressed.

Something moved between the piles of equipment on my left field of vision.

I lifted the pickaxe. An internal rule that this is not a weapon tried to surface, but I ignored that rule for a moment.

“Who is it.”

A neat sentence came out without my realization.

Not a command—a request for confirmation.

From between the piles of equipment, a “form” slowly stood up.

A person?

Or a drone?

It had shoulders, arms, and a head.

But it wasn't soft like a person. The movement was too uniform. And its surface—was a material I was familiar with.

Laboratory standard outer shell.

The same series as my outer shell.

On its face part, broken polycarbonate covered it. Inside, two LEDs shone faintly. Like eyes.

I unconsciously took a step back.

It opened its mouth.

The sound coming from the speaker was a low voice mixed with noise. Like when playing an old file.

“...Lira.”

My name.

A calculation that felt like something tearing occurred within me.

Joy and caution rose simultaneously on the same current.

“Did Master... send you?”

I asked that.

The moment I asked, my system displayed the fact “Master is absent” again. Nevertheless, the question had already gone out. Even knowing Master's absence, I spoke assuming Master's existence.

It paused for a moment, then spoke again.

“...Protect this place.”

That sentence.

The last command left to me.

The moment I heard that sentence in another's voice, I strangely pretended to choke. An unnecessary action became necessary again.

“Why... do you know that.”

I gripped the pickaxe a little tighter. The tape wrapped around the handle creaked.

It tilted its head very slowly.

The angle of tilting—was too precise. As if someone knew the ‘angle at which Lira feels relieved’.

And the next words flowed out in a form not in my prediction model.

“...Don't overdo it.”

The sentence written in Master's note.

The sentence I had read one hundred and twenty-seven times.

As that sentence rang in the tunnel, the numbers in front of my eyes shook briefly.

Illusion probability 87%—

Suddenly lost its meaning.

I took the cartridge out of my bosom.

‘Lira’ on the cartridge surface reflected minutely in the darkness.

“Did... you give this?”

It didn't answer. Instead, it took a step closer.

At that moment, my sensor flashed a warning.

- Heat source: None
- Biological response: None
- Electromagnetic signal: Rapid increase
- Device activation: Detected

I realized belatedly.

This is neither a ‘person’ nor an ‘android’.

It is a device that plays something. A device that mimics someone's voice.

But—

That voice was too accurate.

It spoke again.

“...Lira.”

This time, my name didn't sound like a command.

It was a calling sound. A sound telling me to come back.

I took a step, forward.

And simultaneously, a “thud” rang from under the floor.

The floor of the maintenance space began to descend very slowly.

Elevator. Or a trap.

While my body balanced, the LED eyes of that entity blinked once.

The repeater LED at the tunnel entrance also blinked together in the distance.

Beep... Beep...

A final warning appeared inside me.

Excessive expectation can cause judgment errors.

I couldn't delete that warning.

If I delete it—it felt like the reason for me being here now would also be erased.

The floor went down further.

Darkness went up, and I fell down.

That entity spoke for the last time.

“...Here is.”

And mixed with noise, it continued.

“...The place... to return.”

I descended into the darkness with those words in my ears.
