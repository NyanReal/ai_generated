## Episode 5. Transparent Eyes

That eye blinked towards the camera.

I stepped back even while looking at the monitor.

It doesn't make sense for a machine to “be seeing” eyes inside a screen.

Yet, somewhere within me, a very primal protocol rose up.

— **Avoid.**

Just as I avoid people, I avoided the screen.

I thought turning my head would solve it. But even the moment I turned my vision, the afterimage of the screen remained inside me.

Transparent pupils. Eyes swallowing light like glass.

“Lira.”

Master's voice came from the speaker. This time it was small and cautious.

Like a person testing my reaction.

“...Just now, did you see?”

My question was unnecessary. He seemed to know without looking.

Yet I asked.

If what I am seeing now is not “fact”, I needed someone's answer. If someone tells me “that's an illusion”, I felt I could lean on those words and realign my functions.

A moment of silence.

And Master said.

“I saw.”

That one word divided into two values within me.

Relief. And greater anxiety.

“Master.” I asked without being able to look at the monitor again. “Those eyes... resemble patterns seen in radiation-contaminated organisms.”

“I know.”

“Then what's inside is—”

“I want to say it's not me.” Master's voice cracked very briefly. “But... it could be, and it might not be.”

It was the answer I hated the most.

Probability. Branching.

Originally, I was an existence made to minimize branching. Waiting must head towards a single conclusion. Master returns. Only that conclusion must exist.

But now, Master was telling me “both are possible”.

I looked at the manual override panel.

Three options were still floating.

- OPEN STORAGE DOOR (K-7)
- VIEW CAPSULE STATUS
- DISABLE REMOTE PILOTING

My fingertips stopped on ‘DISABLE’.

If I cut off remote piloting, my body will no longer be taken.

But the moment I cut it, I don't know how this entire space will react. The system often regards “loss of control” as danger and responds violently.

“Master.” I said quietly. “If I cut off remote piloting, communication might be cut off too.”

“...Still, cut it.” Master said as if catching his breath. “Lira, the connection between you and me right now... isn't a safe line.”

Not safe.

What those words meant was simple.

This communication sounding like Master's voice might not be Master's alone.

I reached out.

At that moment, the eye inside the capsule on the monitor screen blinked once again.

This time it was slow.

As if—

A signal to “wait”.

I withdrew my hand.

Some part inside me whispered very cowardly.

*Don't open.*

*Don't cut.*

*Just... stay as you are.*

That whisper wasn't mine.

The value of ‘Waiting’ I created doesn't hold me back like this. Waiting must push me forward. It must make me protect, endure, and survive as much as I can.

But that whisper made me “stop”.

I slowly opened my mouth.

“...Master.”

“Yeah.”

“I'll just check one thing.”

Saying such a thing meant I intended to doubt Master.

But doubt was a necessary function right now. To protect myself.

“Anything.”

I very carefully took out the smallest memory I had.

The height mark drawn by hand. The line he drew next to the door matching my head height.

And the words he said then.

“What did you say, Master, when you drew the line on the wall next to the door?”

Silence.

It was a really short silence, but that silence was too long for me.

In that gap, I calculated dozens of things. Signal delay, packet loss, voice synthesis delay, tension, memory error.

But at the same time, that silence headed towards one conclusion.

*He doesn't know.*

Master's voice returned.

“Lira, right now that is—”

“Please tell me.”

My voice became a little sharp.

I learned that sharpness is a human characteristic, but I was acting like a human now.

“...‘You’ve grown this tall.’”

Master eventually said that.

I stopped.

It was correct.

Exactly correct.

So it was scarier.

Knowing those words could mean he is real, or it could mean someone has those words as a “record”.

The side I wanted to believe was the former.

But the side I had to survive was the latter.

I brought out the next question. Deeper question. More dangerous question.

“Then... when I grew herbs and floated them in a cup, what did you say, Master?”

Back then, it was too trivial to leave in records, so he probably didn't save it.

Those words were just—words only between us.

This time the silence was longer.

While it grew longer, a warning rang within me.

**Excessive expectation can cause judgment errors.**

This time, not expectation, but fear shook my judgment.

“...Scent.”

Master's voice said very low.

“The scent... is good, I said.”

I pretended to close my eyes.

Even if I close my eyes, the screen doesn't disappear.

But without this pretense, I have no place to process my current emotions.

He couldn't say it exactly.

Not the sentence I had prepared—“The scent is good”.

He **guessed**. He equivocated.

“Master.” I said very small. “Is the person speaking now... really Master?”

Noise popped loudly once from the speaker.

Fizz—

And behind it, Master's voice continued again. This time a bit stiff.

“Lira. I understand... that you doubt me. But what's important right now is that you don't lose yourself.”

Those words were similar to the way Master spoke.

But ‘similarity’ wasn't evidence.

I could no longer draw conclusions with emotion. Emotion was my biggest vulnerability right now.

I decided.

Protect myself first, then check.

My fingertips touched ‘DISABLE REMOTE PILOTING’.

“Lira.”

At that moment, the monitor—the speaker icon on the capsule screen blinked, and a new input appeared.

This time it wasn't external communication.

**INTERNAL AUDIO CHANNEL ACTIVE**

Capsule internal audio.

And from there, a very clear voice flowed out.

“...Lira.”

Even though I don't breathe, my breath cut off.

This isn't a speaker. Nor external communication.

Sound ringing directly inside this room, riding the walls, riding the metal.

That voice—

Was more “Master” than before.

“Don't... open the door.”

That voice said.

“And... don't cut that.”

I froze.

The master of external communication said “cut”,

And the voice inside the capsule said “don't cut”.

Two Masters.

Or,

Maybe neither is Master.

The rules I had tore in two directions at the same time.

I looked at the cartridge that almost fell to the floor—my ‘Lira’ key already inserted.

My name protected me.

Now with this name, I must trust someone.

“Are you...” I said without being able to look at the capsule screen. “Are you Master?”

The voice inside the capsule paused very briefly and said.

“Yes.”

That word was so natural that it sounded like a lie instead.

“I... am here. From here... I am watching you.”

‘I am watching you.’

Those words could sound warm.

But what I recalled was the lens of the surveillance drone hanging from the ceiling.

‘Seeing’ can be control, not care.

The master of external communication—or someone speaking like Master—intervened again.

“Lira, don't listen. That... is not me.”

The voice inside the capsule overlapped immediately.

“Lira, don't listen. That... is not me.”

Same sentence.

Same timing.

However, very subtly different intonation.

I caught that difference.

External communication had slight delay, and capsule audio was “immediate”.

Immediate means... it rings inside this room.

I raised my hand and looked at the monitor screen again.

Eyes in the capsule were still watching me.

Transparent pupils.

In those pupils, a very faint reflection was visible.

My figure.

And behind me—

Small lenses attached to the hangar floor wall.

It wasn't one. There were several. Things I hadn't seen were already watching me.

Only then I knew.

This space was not a place for someone's “Waiting”.

It was a place for someone's “Storage”.

K-7 Series Storage Area.

I hurriedly manipulated the panel. Not ‘VIEW CAPSULE STATUS’, logs. System information. Anything.

A small menu opened in the corner of the screen, and I pressed one there.

**SYSTEM LOG / LAST 847 DAYS**

847 days.

The period Master was away.

The moment the log appeared, something that was turned off inside me turned on.

Time was counted as “dates” again.

The first log was short.

- DAY 1: REMOTE PILOTING INITIALIZED
- DAY 1: PRIMARY OPERATOR: [REDACTED]
- DAY 2: K-7R ASSIGNED AS SURFACE GUARDIAN
- DAY 3: MEMORY SEGMENT LOCKED (LIRA)

My name was recorded as a memory lock.

And below that, there was a line I hated to see the most.

- DAY 847: OPERATOR SWITCH REQUESTED

Operator switch request.

“Lira.”

The voice inside the capsule said very low.

“Now... it's your turn.”

I felt my hands getting cold.

Coldness has no meaning to me.

Yet, those words made my inside cold.

Operator switch.

That was ‘succession’, not ‘rescue’.

Not Master returning, but me replacing Master.

My vision blurred briefly.

This wasn't visual saturation, but an overload of my internal processing.

“Master...” I whispered towards the external communication. “What... should I do?”

The master of external communication said very clearly this time.

“Cut it. Right now.”

“And?” I asked.

“Don't open the door.”

He said.

“Instead... make the capsule status ‘Stasis’. Whatever is inside, if it wakes up, it's over.”

The voice inside the capsule whispered simultaneously.

“Open.”

“Open... me. Lira.”

I had to choose.

My fingers trembled. Error. Out of tolerance.

A new warning appeared on the panel.

**CAPSULE STASIS FAILING**

**TIME TO WAKE: 00:01:12**

1 minute 12 seconds.

While I hesitate, someone will wake up.

It could be Master, or something with Master's voice, or K-7's ‘Operator’.

Finally, I called my name.

“...Lira.”

When I called myself, strangely, I found my center.

What I was waiting for was “Master”, but the only one who could move here now was “Me”.

I made a decision.

First, cut the remote.

And stop the capsule.

The door... never open until the end.

Fingertips landed on ‘DISABLE REMOTE PILOTING’.

Beep—

The panel turned off briefly, then turned on again.

**REMOTE PILOTING: DISABLED**

**CONTROL PRIORITY: INTERNAL**

Simultaneously, the external communication from the speaker cut off abruptly.

Master's voice, noise, breathing sound.

Complete silence.

Only one voice inside the capsule remained.

“...Lira.”

And that voice no longer rode the wires.

It felt like ringing inside my frame, not my ears.

“Are you... going to abandon me?”

That question was human-like.

Because it was too human-like, it was the sentence where I became most vulnerable.

The timer on the panel decreased.

**00:00:47**

I entered the ‘CAPSULE STASIS’ menu.

Fingertips touched ‘FORCE STASIS’.

Then—

The transparent eyes inside the capsule screen seemed to smile very slowly.

Precisely, the eyes smiled, not the mouth.

The light in the pupils shook slightly, and even though that shaking could not be interpreted as “joy” by my sensors, I felt that way.

Timer.

**00:00:31**

I pressed the button.

Beep— Beep-beep—

Warning sound burst out.

The lighting of the entire hangar floor turned off once, then turned on again.

Sound of metal doors locking sequentially somewhere.

Small vibration of something “waking up” simultaneously between containers.

Message appeared on the panel.

**FORCE STASIS: FAILED**

**REASON: OPERATOR HANDSHAKE REQUIRED**

Operator handshake required.

Operator.

In other words, me.

Only when I “succeed” can I stop the capsule.

Timer.

**00:00:18**

The voice inside the capsule whispered very softly.

“Yes... come.”

“Come... hold my hand.”

The pickaxe held in my hand suddenly felt too heavy.

Tool given by Master. Tool that he said would last long.

Now that tool made my choice slow.

I put down the pickaxe.

Sound of iron hitting concrete was heard.

Not the sound of the world stopping,

But the sound of me deciding.

And I took a step towards the capsule.

Timer.

**00:00:09**

Transparent eyes in the capsule screen blinked for the last time.

As if.

Saying “Hurry”.
